A traffic light that learns when to switch.

Fixed-time and actuated signals waste green on empty approaches. This project turns the paper Adaptive Traffic Signal Control using a Dueling Double Deep Q-Network (Mirza, Mujawar, Ansari, Dethe, Khandke; Vidyalankar Institute of Technology) into something you can drive: run an intersection, swap controllers, and compare.

Every controller obeys the paper's safety wrapper: 10 s minimum green, 3 s yellow, 2 s all-red, decisions every 5 s. The "D3QN-style" policy is a hand-coded stand-in that scores waiting pressure; it is not the trained network.

Results reported in the paper

SUMO 1.27.1, 1200 s episodes, mean over seeds 1, 2, 3. Throughput was statistically indistinguishable across controllers: the gain is less idling, not more vehicles served.

Mixed traffic and PCU weighting

A bus takes up far more road than a motorcycle. Counting each as one vehicle misleads the agent, so state and reward use IRC 106-1990 Passenger Car Units: car 1.0, motorcycle 0.5, auto-rickshaw 0.8, bus 3.0.

Limits and reproducibility

Single isolated intersection, simulation only, permissive left turns, 80 to 150 training episodes. On mixed traffic the agent does not yet beat actuated control. Emissions are simulator estimates. Code, trained models, and experiment scripts: github.com/ali0786mehdi/Intelligent-Traffic-Signal-Controller.
